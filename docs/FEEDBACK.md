Every complaint, critique and preference from this project, in one place, grouped by what they have in common. Written October 16, 2026. Newest decisions win where two conflict (see "Reversals" at the end).

How to read it: each item says what you disliked, then what you asked for. The "Patterns" section at the top is the short version. Use it as a checklist when looking at any page.

## Patterns (the short version)

1. **Paragraphs are never display size.** If it is a sentence or paragraph, set it at reading size. Big type is for short headings only. (Idea paragraph, About header, name note, premise.)
2. **Say only what is true today.** No plans stated as facts, no hedging words. (25 meetups, "roughly", "under a partner's name", "brand", old December numbers as the lead.)
3. **No decoration without a job.** A label, chip or line that does not tell the reader something should go. ("Los Angeles" above the headline, labels that repeat the heading under them.)
4. **Everything has to be easy to read and easy to see.** Heavier text in boxes, bigger section titles, real contrast between sections, nothing "bleak", nothing that blends in.
5. **It has to feel polished and smooth.** No jagged or cheap-looking boxes, smooth motion, a slight shine is welcome.
6. **Controls go where the eye expects.** Primary action first and larger, controls centered, one clear box instead of many small ones.
7. **Facts, not opinions, about people.** Bios are accomplishments about the person. The role is above the bio, not inside it. Same shape for everyone.
8. **Be consistent between people and between pages.** If one person shows school, year, and major, everyone does. If one bio mentions Meridian, none do.
9. **We are a company, not only an event.** The home page says what Meridian is. The conference tab carries the event detail.
10. **Important things sit high.** Team, press, proof near the top, not buried.
11. **Do not remove information that was not asked to be removed.**
12. **Plain words.** Vague phrases ("built with you", "keeps going") read as filler.
